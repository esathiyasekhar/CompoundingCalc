01

About the calculator

The Compounding Calculator projects how a long-term equity portfolio grows when it is split into a steady core and a faster-growing satellite, and how much of the final money comes from compounding rather than from what you put in.

It answers four questions, one on each page:

PageThe question it answers
Core engineIf I invest this lump sum and this monthly SIP, split this way, what will I have in n years?
Goal & scheduleWhen does my plan reach my goal, what SIP or return would get there on time, and what does every year look like?
Annual reviewI have real results for the years so far. Am I still on track, and what must change from here?
Youngster labHow much is starting at 21 worth compared with starting at 24, 27 or 30?

The core + satellite idea

Most of the money (by default 70%) goes into a core of steady compounders expected to earn a moderate return (15% a year by default). The rest (30%) goes into a satellite of higher-growth ideas expected to earn more (25%). The calculator grows the two as separate pots and never rebalances them, so over long periods the satellite usually ends up as most of the wealth.

Projections, not promises

Every result assumes the same return every year. Real markets are uneven, and a bad year early on changes the outcome. Use the Annual review page each year to replace assumptions with what actually happened.

02

Installing and opening

Windows app

  1. Run CompoundingCalculator-Setup.exe. No administrator rights are needed; it installs only for your Windows user.
  2. Click Yes to install (or to update an older version). Your users and records are kept during an update.
  3. Open Compounding Calculator from the Start menu or the desktop. It opens in its own window using Microsoft Edge (or Chrome if Edge is missing).
Program folder
%LOCALAPPDATA%\Programs\Compounding Calculator
Data folder
C:\Users\<you>\Compounding Calculator Data, created for an optional database file (see chapter 10)
Uninstall
Windows Settings → Apps → Installed apps → Compounding Calculator → Uninstall, or run Uninstall.exe in the program folder. The data folder is left in place.

Web page

Open the link you were given. Nothing is installed. Whatever you save stays in that browser on that device, and nobody else can see it.

Your first five minutes

  1. On Core engine, enter your lump sum and monthly SIP, then move the Core allocation, Core CAGR and Satellite CAGR sliders. The − and + beside every slider move it one step (0.5% for returns, 5% for the split, 1 year for years); hold one down to keep going.
  2. Read the large figure: your corpus in the final year. Hover over the chart to see any year.
  3. Press New user, enter your name, a user ID and a goal, then Create user.
  4. Open Goal & schedule to see when you reach the goal and the SIP that gets there on time.
  5. Press Save whenever you want the current plan kept for this user.
Help is built in

Press Help in the header (or F1) on any page. This manual opens at the chapter for that page. The Design manual is one click away in the same window.

Pages

The four tabs under the header are Core engine, Goal & schedule, Annual review and Youngster lab. All pages work from the same plan, so a change on Core engine shows up on the other pages at once.

Inflation, INF_ADJ and Presets

On the right of the tabs: the Inflation rate (default 6%), the INF_ADJ ON / OFF button that shows every page after inflation (chapter 5), and Presets, a menu of ready-made demonstrations for talks (chapter 4).

Reset page

Reset page (right of the tabs) affects only the page you are on, and a message with Undo appears for a few seconds:

  • Core engine: back to the standard plan (₹1,00,000 lump sum, ₹10,000 SIP, 10% step-up, 70/30, 15% / 25%, 30 years, spouse off).
  • Goal & schedule: back to the selected user's saved goal.
  • Annual review: discards entries you typed but did not save. Saved records are untouched.
  • Youngster lab: back to age 20, ₹10,000 a month, 10% step-up for 10 years, SIP for 10 years, 70% / 18% / 25%, wealth after 30, 40 and 50 years.

04

Presets

Ready-made demonstrations for a talk or a slide. One click sets every input, opens the right page and shows a short talking point.

Using a preset

  1. Click Presets (left of Reset page) and pick one. The arrow keys and Enter work too.
  2. The calculator opens the right page. A gold note under the tabs says what was set and what to point at.
  3. Use Next › and ‹ Previous on the note to step through the presets in order, like slides.
  4. Close the note with × when you are done. Undo in the message at the bottom puts back what you had before the preset.
  5. Each preset also sets the INF_ADJ view: off for all of them except the last one, which shows its result after inflation.
Your own data

While a preset note is shown, the Core engine and Goal & schedule pages leave the selected user's year-end records out, so the demonstration shows the plain numbers. Close the note, or open a user, to bring them back. A preset changes the inputs on screen only. Don't press Save unless you want the preset kept as that user's plan; Reset page goes back to the standard plan.

The presets

“Plain” means 100% core at one rate. “70 : 30” keeps the same blended CAGR but splits it 70% core and 30% satellite, never rebalanced, which is why the split versions end higher. The presets always use the same pairs: 26% = core 20% + satellite 40%, 18% = core 15% + satellite 25%, and ≈20% = core 18% + satellite 25%.

05

Inflation and today’s ₹

A crore in 30 years will not buy what a crore buys today. Set an inflation rate once, then see any page in today’s rupees, or make your goal an inflation-adjusted freedom number.

The inflation rate

The rate (0–15%, default 6%) is remembered in this browser and used by both features below. There are four ways to change it:

  • type it in the Inflation box on the right of the tabs;
  • use the − and + beside the box (0.5% at a time);
  • with INF_ADJ on, drag the slider or click a 4%–8% chip in the strip under the tabs;
  • with “Target in today’s money” on, click a chip beside the goal’s inflation line.

INF_ADJ: every page after inflation

Press INF_ADJ OFF so it reads INF_ADJ ON. The button turns gold and a dashed strip under the tabs says the view is on. Every future amount on the Core engine, Goal & schedule and Youngster lab is divided by (1 + inflation) for each year until it happens, so it shows what that money would buy now. Returns become real returns. Press the button again, or INF_ADJ OFF in the strip, to switch back.

A real return is (1 + return) ÷ (1 + inflation) − 1: 18% with 6% inflation is 11.32% in buying power. What you put in is also shown in today’s rupees, because later SIPs cost less in today’s money. The Annual review stays in actual rupees, because that is what you record from your statements.

Goal in today’s money

On Goal & schedule, switch on Target in today’s money under the target. The target then means today’s buying power and grows with inflation: a line under it shows what it becomes by the deadline. The three answers (when the plan gets there, the SIP needed, the CAGR needed) and the CAGR table all use the inflation-adjusted target. The switch is saved with the user's goal.

Freedom number

Slide 13 sets the freedom number at 25 × yearly spending (₹3 Cr for ₹12 lakh a year). With inflation on, ₹3 Cr today becomes ₹9.62 Cr by 60 at 6%: ₹25 lakh at 40 growing at 18% gets there at 63 instead of 55. Presets 20 and 21 show both.

Why IRR is higher than Blended CAGR

The pots are never rebalanced, so the faster satellite becomes a larger share of the money every year (30% of each rupee invested, but about 79% of the corpus by year 30). The return on the whole portfolio drifts up towards the satellite's rate. The note under the two figures states the gap in points.

Growth path chart

Lines show the total corpus, the core, the satellite and the money invested (dashed). Linear shows rupees to scale. Log makes each doubling the same height, which makes the early years readable.

The gold markers show when 25%, 50% and 75% of the final corpus has been made. The line above the chart says it in words: with the standard plan, half of the year-30 corpus is made by year 26.6, so the last 3.4 years make the other half.

With your spouse

When the spouse switch is on, a strip under the chart compares Self only with With spouse: both corpora, both amounts invested, the extra wealth from the spouse's money, and how much sooner the self-only corpus is reached together. The tooltip adds a Self only line.

08

Annual review

Once a year, record what you actually invested and what the portfolio is worth. The calculator re-plans the rest of the road to the goal.

Choose the user

Type a name or user ID in Find a user by name or user ID and press Open (or pick from the header). The user card shows the goal, the year the plan started, how many years are recorded and when the user was last saved. Edit user & goal changes the name, goal or start year.

The four cards

Projected at goal year
Recorded years as they actually happened, then the plan for the rest. Says how far above or short of the goal this lands.
Goal reached in
The year the revised projection reaches the goal, before or after the deadline.
Return needed from here on
The blended CAGR needed for the remaining years, compared with your planned CAGR.
Monthly SIP needed from year n
The SIP from next year on that reaches the goal at the planned returns. If your SIP is still running (including the extra months of its last year), the planned SIP path (with its step-up) is scaled up or down. If it has already ended, the card shows a flat SIP restarted until the goal year. Past years are never changed.

Recording a year

  1. At the end of each plan year, find that year's row in Year-end records. The grey placeholders show the planned figures.
  2. Enter Actual invested (leave blank to use the planned amount), Core value and Satellite value at year end. Both values are needed.
  3. Press Save on the row (or Enter). The record is stored for the user straight away, and the next year's row appears.

Return achieved is the year's growth divided by the money at work: opening value plus half of the year's investments. It is shown for the whole portfolio and for core and satellite separately. A row's Delete removes that year; Delete all records clears the user's history.

Records also move the Core engine

Once a user has records, the Core engine and Goal & schedule pages start from the recorded values too, so all pages tell the same story.

The chart compares the original plan, the actual + revised projection and the goal line.

How far ahead to look

The three cards show the wealth after three horizons, in years from today (default 30, 40 and 50 years: ages 50, 60 and 70 for a 20-year-old). Two ways to change them:

  • All three together: the Show wealth after slider, or its − and +, moves the three horizons by the same number of years. The line beside it shows the horizons and the ages.
  • One at a time: the small − and + on each card move just that card by a year. The cards stay in order, and the last one can reach age 100.

Results

Each card shows the age, the years from today, the wealth and the multiple of money invested. Below: the total invested, the amount made by compounding and the cost of waiting. With the defaults: ₹24.01 Cr at 50, ₹189.88 Cr at 60 and ₹1,591.63 Cr at 70 from ₹19.12 L invested.

What waiting costs

The same lump sum and SIP started later, measured at the last age. Wait in steps of chooses 3, 5 or 10 years (so 3/6/9, 5/10/15 or 10/20/30 years later). With the defaults, waiting 3 years costs about half the final wealth (−50.5%), 6 years −75.4% and 9 years −87.7%.

How fast money doubles

A small table compares a fixed deposit (6.5%), the Nifty 50 index (13%) and your Core+SAT mix from the three sliders: the return, the doubling time and what ₹1 lakh becomes in 30 years. The note under it gives the mix's first doubling time and how many times a lump sum doubles before the last age.

Why the 70 : 30 figures are so large

The core and the satellite grow as separate pots with no rebalancing, as in the Core engine. Over decades the faster satellite ends up as nearly all the money, so the result is much higher than one flat rate would give. For the defaults (≈20% blended), a flat 20% gives ₹698.05 Cr at 70 instead of ₹1,591.63 Cr. Set Core allocation to 100% and Core CAGR to 20% to see it.

Year-by-year table

Click Show to see every year from your age to the last horizon: age, year, monthly SIP, invested and growth that year, total invested, wealth and multiple. The three card ages are tagged. With INF_ADJ on, every figure on this page is in today’s rupees.

10

Users and saving

Each user holds one plan, one goal and their year-end records. Users work like a small database kept on your computer.

Creating a user

Name
Shown in the user list and on reports.
User ID
A unique short ID: 2–32 characters of lowercase letters, digits, dot, dash or underscore (e.g. sekhar or priya.k). It is suggested from the name.
Goal
Target corpus, the number of years, and the year the plan started (year 1).
Start the plan from
Current inputs copies the Core engine as it is now; Standard plan uses ₹1 L + ₹10k SIP, 15% / 25%.

Saving

Plan and goal changes are kept for a user only when you press Save. Until then the button is gold and the storage pill says • unsaved. Year-end records are different: each row is saved when you press its own Save. Switching user with unsaved changes asks first.

Where data is kept

Users are always saved in the browser the calculator runs in. In the Windows app you can also link a database file (Manage → Database file), which is rewritten after every change and survives browser resets:

  • On this PC only: create the file in Compounding Calculator Data in your user folder. Nothing leaves your computer.
  • Synced: create it in your OneDrive folder to use the same users on another PC. Open the same file there with Open database file….

When two copies differ, the more recently updated version of each user wins, and deleted users stay deleted. After restarting Windows the pill may say Reconnect <file>; click it and allow access.

Backups

Export all users (JSON) saves every user to one file; Import users (JSON) merges a file back in using the same newer-wins rule. Export before reinstalling Windows or moving to a new PC if you do not use a database file.

11

Reports and exports

PDF report

  1. Press PDF in the header.
  2. Choose Summary (1 page): assumptions, headline numbers, growth chart and doubling milestones; or Summary + 60-year schedule, which adds the full table with headers repeated on every page.
  3. Press Open print dialog, choose Save as PDF and turn on Background graphics for colours.

The report header shows the investor (the selected user), the date, the horizon, the version and the developer.

CSV and copying

CSV in the header, or Download CSV in the schedule, saves the 60-year schedule for Excel. Copy table copies it so you can paste it into a spreadsheet or email.

The web edition cannot download files or open the print dialog, so these buttons appear only in the Windows app.

Against a goal of ₹10 Cr in 20 years

The plan has ₹3.13 Cr at year 20 and reaches ₹10 Cr in year 25.9. To get there on time you would need either a starting SIP of ₹34,996 a month (same step-up and stop years), or a blended CAGR of 26.8% with today's SIP.

Questions and fixes

My corpus changed after I entered a year-end record.

That is intended. Recorded years replace the plan for that user on every page. Open a user with no records, or delete the records, to see the pure plan.

Why is the Portfolio IRR different from the Blended CAGR?

Because the satellite is not rebalanced back to 30%, it becomes a growing share of the portfolio. See chapter 6.

The Goal card says “Not reachable”.

The SIP stops before the deadline, so no starting SIP gets there in time. Increase SIP runs for on the Core engine, extend the deadline, or lower the target.

The storage pill says “Reconnect …”.

Windows needs your permission again to edit the database file. Click the pill and choose Allow. Until then changes are kept in the browser and written to the file once reconnected.

My users are missing.

Users live in the browser that saved them. If you opened the calculator in a different browser or profile, use the database file or Import users (JSON) from a backup.

In the web edition, Delete does nothing.

Pop-up confirmations are blocked there. Click the same Delete button again within 6 seconds to confirm.

The months option is on SIP but not on step-up.

By design: the SIP can end part-way through a year, while the step-up changes only once a year.

After a preset my year-end records seem to be ignored.

They are, on purpose, while the preset note is shown, so the demonstration shows plain numbers. Close the note with × or open the user again to bring them back. The Annual review page always uses them.

Why did every number drop when I turned INF_ADJ on?

The view divides future money by inflation, so it shows buying power today. Nothing changed in the plan; press INF_ADJ OFF to see actual rupees again.

Can returns be negative?

Recorded years can show negative returns. Planned returns are always within the slider ranges.

Glossary

Lakh, crore
₹1 lakh (L) = ₹1,00,000. ₹1 crore (Cr) = ₹1,00,00,000 = 100 lakh. Amounts are grouped the Indian way.
Corpus
The total value of the portfolio at a point in time.
Lump sum
Money invested once, at the start.
SIP
Systematic investment plan: a fixed amount invested every month.
Step-up
The yearly increase in the monthly SIP, in % or ₹.
Core
The larger, steadier part of the portfolio.
Satellite
The smaller, higher-growth part of the portfolio.
CAGR
Compound annual growth rate: the steady yearly return assumed for a pot.
Blended CAGR
The allocation-weighted average of core and satellite CAGR.
IRR
Internal rate of return: the one yearly return that links every rupee put in, at the time it went in, to the final corpus.
Horizon
The year results are measured at.
Multiple
Corpus divided by the total amount invested.
Doubling time
Years for money to double at a given return: ln 2 ÷ ln(1 + return).
Rule of 72
A quick estimate of doubling time: 72 ÷ return in %.
Inflation
The yearly rise in prices. Set it in the tab bar; default 6%.
Today’s rupees
A future amount divided by (1 + inflation) for each year until then: what it would buy now.
Real return
Return after inflation: (1 + return) ÷ (1 + inflation) − 1.
Return achieved
In the Annual review: a year's growth divided by the opening value plus half the year's investments.
